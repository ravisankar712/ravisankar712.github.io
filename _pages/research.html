Quantum Field Theory is one of our best frameworks to understand the world. At heart, quantum field theory allows us to construct quantum mechanical models of particles interacting through local forces. Be it fundamental particles like electrons or emergent quasi particles in solids, quantum field theory turns out to be the right framework to use. One of the great things about working on Quantum Field Theory is that it allows us to sample a variety of different interesting topics in physics, ranging from elementary particles to gravity to condensed matter, and even to pure mathematics.

Below listed are the themes of research I have done so far. I hope to deepen and broaden the list even more in the future!

Classical Gravitational Observables from Amplitudes

With Prof. Diana Vaman, Sid Ajith and Yuchen Du, we used the first quantized version of quantum field theory (called worldline quantum field theory) to systematically construct the classical limit of gravitational and electromagnetic two body scattering. We showed that the worldline formalism and the QFT based methods are equivalent. Using this equivalence, we can extract the classical scattering potential directly in a diagrammatic way. We were also able to check a conjecture that exists in the QFT based method to all orders in the perturbative expansion of the potential, where previous results only went up to three orders.
